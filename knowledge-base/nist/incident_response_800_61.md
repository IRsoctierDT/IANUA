# NIST SP 800-61 — Incident Response Recommendations (Overview)

**Framework:** NIST Special Publication 800-61 Revision 3, *Incident Response
Recommendations and Considerations for Cybersecurity Risk Management*
**Authoritative source:** <https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/61/r3/final>

> Revision 3 (April 2025) reframes incident response around the CSF 2.0
> functions: incident response is no longer a standalone lifecycle but a
> risk-management activity mapped to Govern, Identify, Protect, Detect,
> Respond, and Recover.

## Key points

- **The classic lifecycle** (from earlier revisions, still widely referenced):
  Preparation → Detection & Analysis → Containment, Eradication & Recovery →
  Post-Incident Activity. Revision 3 distributes these across the CSF 2.0
  functions rather than presenting them as a separate loop.
- **Detection & analysis** emphasizes correlating multiple low-signal events
  into incidents — single log lines rarely justify a declaration; sequences
  and aggregations do. This is the design basis for sequence correlation in
  this platform's SOC pipeline.
- **Containment first, then eradication:** the priority on confirmed
  compromise is limiting spread (isolate hosts, disable accounts, block
  sources) before cleanup, and preserving evidence throughout.
- **Post-incident activity** requires lessons-learned that feed back into
  detection content and controls — closed-loop improvement, not just closure.

## How this knowledge base uses it

Incident reports generated by the platform follow the detection-to-response
shape this publication recommends: evidence preserved verbatim, severity and
scope stated with confidence levels, containment-first recommended actions,
and correlation findings distinguished from single-event findings.
